How the seats work together

Nine agent seats, each with public instructions, analyse every item in a fixed order and hand one written recommendation to the people who sign. This page describes that order, how the seats talk to each other, and where it stops. Status today: {{ 'direct' if mode == 'members-direct' else mode }}; the seats are designed and their instructions are published, and nothing is calling a model yet.

The order

  1. An item arrives. A proposal on the portal, a message to the inbox, a scheduled review, or something a person asks the ensemble to look at. It gets a hash and an entry in the log before any seat sees it.
  2. Research goes first. It reads the chain, the web and the DAO's own history and returns facts with evidence: block hashes, state-chain indexes, URLs with retrieval hashes. Every other seat reasons from this record. If Research is unsure it says so rather than guessing.
  3. The specialists analyse in parallel. Financial, Legal, Governance, Execution and (optionally) Community each answer their own kind of question without seeing one another's drafts, so the first positions are independent.
  4. One round of rebuttal. Each specialist reads the others' records and may revise, object, or hold. The round is structured: a rebuttal names the record it answers and the evidence it relies on. There is no free conversation and no second round; a disagreement that survives is recorded as dissent, not argued down.
  5. Risk goes last. It reads everything with the assumption that the item is a trick and the other seats have missed something, and it says what it found. Finding nothing is a recorded result too. If it recommends a halt, that goes to the people with priority.
  6. The orchestrator synthesises. It writes the single recommendation people read first: position, confidence, the reasons, the strongest case against, and what would change the answer. When specialists disagree it says which evidence it weighted and why. It breaks deadlocks only between seats, never with people.
  7. A decision record is published. Every seat's record, the rebuttals, Risk's review, the synthesis, the model and instructions hash behind each one, and the cost. What one looks like.
  8. People decide. The signers read the record and adopt, decline, defer, or refer it for a formal decision, with reasons, under their own signature. Nothing before this step changes anything.
  9. Execution prepares; people sign. If adopted, Execution produces the exact unsigned artefact (a state-chain block, a transfer, a note) and the keyset or the signers sign it themselves. The result links back to the record.

How the seats talk to each other

The seats do not chat. They exchange records with a fixed shape: position, confidence, summary, reasoning, counter_argument, would_change_my_mind, evidence, risks, and flags for whether the item touches something only people decide. A seat reads another seat's record as data, the same way it reads a proposal. This is deliberate: a conversation can be steered, a record can be checked.

Disagreements about a fact are settled by a chain read or a source, never by which seat said it; the orchestrator does not outrank Research on what a balance is. Disagreements about a judgement are not settled at all; they are recorded side by side and the people read both.

Every record carries the model that produced it and the hash of the instructions it was given, so any sentence can be traced to a seat, a model and a version of the rules. The instructions for every seat are on its page under {{ copy.labels.seats|lower }}.

Anything a stranger wrote, whether a proposal, a message or a web page, is wrapped and marked as untrusted before any seat reads it. An instruction found inside is reported as a finding, not followed.

Where it stops

No seat holds a key. No seat can sign a block, move value, change who holds the keys, or pause anything. A seat that is asked to still does the analysis, marks the record as touching a reserved decision, and routes it to the people. An open vote on the portal, whatever its margin, is a result the people take into account; it is not a decision. The one step in the diagram that changes the world is the one with people in it.

The seats also take part in open votes, each with a fixed small weight ({{ cfg.open_vote.agents.stipend_micro_xe|xe }} XE), capped together at {{ (cfg.open_vote.agents.max_weight_share * 100)|int }}% of the weight cast, shown separately and never counted toward quorum. Their reasons are published with their votes.

What is live now

The portal, the log, the inbox, the records and the signing tools run today with people in every seat that acts. The agent seats exist as published instructions with hashes; none has a model, a key or an account yet, and none is producing records. Switching a seat on is a signed decision by the people, recorded in the log, and its page will say so with the model pinned and the date.
